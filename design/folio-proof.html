I.

I · May 8, 1977 · Barton Hall

A hard freeze settled
over Barton Hall.

080577

On Sunday, May 8, 1977 — Mother’s Day — Betty Cantor-Jackson set up her own two-track mix inside the fieldstone drill hall and recorded the Grateful Dead. The tape did not stay in Ithaca. It moved from listener to listener for decades, carrying the performance far beyond the room where it began.c11c17

In 2011 the Library of Congress placed the recording in the National Recording Registry — the only Grateful Dead concert held there — and in 2017 Cornell University Press published a book about the show. The room held 5,000; student tickets cost $6.50 in advance and $7.50 at the door. Outside, the night fell into a hard freeze.c6c34c18c19c20c17

        B.

        Why the OBEL matters

        Effects before volume.

        The TB500 pioneered Garcia’s onboard effects loop. The Mu-Tron received the pickup signal before the guitar’s master volume, so turning down on the instrument did not starve the envelope filter of the level it needed to respond.

        Buffer A unity-gain stage converted the guitar to a low-impedance signal, preserving clarity through the long send-and-return cable run.

        Why this architecture mattered

        Control first.
        Power second.

        Garcia divided the work of a conventional guitar amplifier into separate stages. The TB500 controlled pickup combinations and effect level; the Twin supplied the preamp character; Budman supplied clean power; and the JBLs turned that power into a fast, detailed stage voice.

        • Before volume: the Mu-Tron received a consistent signal and continued tracking Garcia’s picking dynamics.
        • After volume: the Twin shaped the tone while the MC2300 delivered headroom instead of power-tube distortion.
        • At the cabinets: JBL K120s emphasized articulation, transient attack, and the separation between individual notes.
