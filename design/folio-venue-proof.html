I.

Barton Hall · Cornell University · Ithaca, New York

Barton Hall.

Designed by New York State Architect Lewis F. Pilcher, the New York State Armory and Drill Hall opened at Cornell in 1915. Its local-limestone walls enclosed a vast unpillared drill room—an interior large enough to assemble a thousand cadets without columns breaking the floor.

Military drill defined the building’s first decades, but Cornell kept finding new forms of assembly for the same room. Barton became a basketball court, indoor track, examination hall, registration floor, commencement room, and concert venue. In 1940 it took the name of Col. Frank A. Barton, Class of 1891.

Floors, tracks, support rooms, and roofs changed with each generation. The essential architecture did not: a fortress-like stone shell wrapped around one long trussed volume, built to hold a crowd beneath a single roof.

Place record · Barton Hall

One room.
Several lives.

Barton Hall was built in 1915 as New York State’s Armory and Drill Hall. The fieldstone shell and long, uninterrupted span belonged to military formation; Cornell kept finding new uses for all that room.

It became a track, an examination hall, a commencement room, a registration floor, a concert venue, and a place large enough to stage almost anything. Each use left another layer: altered floors, new athletic facilities, repaired roofs, preserved military collections, and generations of Cornell memory.

II.

The Grateful Dead at Barton Hall

The Dead
came once.

The Cornell Concert Commission brought the Grateful Dead into Barton Hall for a single Sunday night in 1977. The performance was one date on a spring tour; the recording made the building’s name travel much farther.

For Deadheads, “Barton Hall” became more than an address. It became a way of naming the circulating two-track, the official release that followed, and the argument over how one night came to stand for an era.
